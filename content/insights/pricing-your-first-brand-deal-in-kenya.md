The most common question in our DMs is some version of "I have 12k followers, what should I charge?" The honest answer is that followers are the least useful input. Here's a way to build a rate you can defend.

## Start from deliverables, not audience

List what the brand is asking for. A typical first deal might be:

- 1 Instagram Reel (30–45s)
- 3 Story frames with a link sticker
- 1 TikTok cut of the same content
- Brand approval of the script before shooting

Each of those is work: a concept, a shoot, an edit, captions, revisions, posting at a specific time. Estimate the hours honestly. Most creators underestimate the edit and the back-and-forth by half.

## Put a day rate on your time

Decide what a day of your work is worth. If you're a photographer who charges KES 25,000 for a half-day shoot, that's your anchor. If you've never charged for anything, look at what a junior in your craft earns at an agency and work back to a day rate — then remember you're also carrying your own equipment, software, transport and taxes.

Multiply the hours by that rate. That's your **production fee**.

## Add what the audience is worth

Now the audience matters. A brand isn't only paying for the video; they're paying to put it in front of the people who trust you. A simple way to value that is engagement, not reach: a 6% engagement rate on 12,000 followers is worth more to most brands than 1% on 100,000.

There's no fixed multiplier, but for a creator with a genuinely engaged niche audience it's reasonable for the audience component to be equal to or larger than the production fee.

## Add usage and exclusivity

If the brand wants to run your content as ads or on their own channels, that's a usage fee on top. If they want you to avoid competing brands for a period, that's exclusivity — you're being paid not to work, so price it like lost work.

## Sanity-check against the market

From campaigns that have run on Zaumu, first deals for Kenyan creators with 5–20k engaged followers most often land between **KES 15,000 and 60,000** for a Reel-plus-stories package, with the spread driven mostly by niche (finance and tech pay more than lifestyle), production quality, and whether usage was included. Treat that as a range to check yourself against, not a rate card.

## Say the number, then stop talking

Send the number with a one-line breakdown — production, audience, usage — and stop. Don't pre-discount. If the brand's budget is lower, they'll tell you, and you can remove deliverables rather than lowering the rate for the same work.
