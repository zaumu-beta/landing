When a brand uses your content in ads, on billboards, or across channels beyond what was agreed, that's a separate fee. Most first-time creators give away usage rights for free without knowing. A post rate covers a post. Usage rights cover everything else. They are not the same thing.

## What "usage" actually means

Every piece of content you make has two lives. The first is the post on your own channel — that's what a brand is paying for when they book "one Instagram Reel". The second is everything the brand might do with that Reel afterwards: run it as a paid ad, put it on their website, cut it into a TV spot, print a still on a billboard along Mombasa Road.

That second life is called **usage** (or licensing), and it has real value to the brand. A Reel that performs well as an ad can save them a production shoot. That value should show up in your invoice.

## The questions to ask before you quote

Before you put a number on a deal, get answers to four things:

1. **Where** will the content be used — your channels only, the brand's organic channels, or paid media?
2. **For how long** — 30 days, 6 months, in perpetuity?
3. **In which markets** — Kenya only, East Africa, global?
4. **Can it be edited** — will they cut it, add their own captions, use a still from it?

"In perpetuity, all media, worldwide" is the most expensive combination and it is also, unhelpfully, the default wording in many contracts. Don't sign it without pricing it.

## How to price it

There is no single formula, but a common starting point is to charge usage as a percentage of your base content fee per period. Many creators price paid-media usage at 20–50% of the base fee per month, and organic reuse on the brand's own channels at a lower rate or bundled in. Longer terms should be discounted per month but larger in total.

What matters more than the exact percentage is that usage appears as its own line. Once it's a line, it can be negotiated. When it's hidden inside "the deal", it's free.
