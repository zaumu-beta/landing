"We need this by tomorrow" is a negotiation tactic, not a real deadline. Urgency is manufactured to stop you from thinking clearly about your rate. Sleep on every deal. A brand that respects you will wait 24 hours. One that won't? That tells you everything.

## Why urgency works on creators

Most creators are solo operators. There's no account manager to say "let me check and come back to you", so the pressure lands directly on the person who also has to make the content. Add the fear that the opportunity disappears if you hesitate, and you get creators quoting on the phone, low, and regretting it by the weekend.

Brands and agencies know this. Their own approval process took weeks; the 24-hour deadline is for you, not for them.

## The script

You don't have to be difficult, you just need one sentence you can send every time:

> "Thanks — this sounds great. Let me review the brief properly and come back to you with a rate by tomorrow at 10am."

That's it. You've shown enthusiasm, you've set a specific time, and you've bought yourself a night to check the usage terms, count the deliverables, and look at what you charged last time.

## What to do with the 24 hours

Write down every deliverable the brief actually asks for, including the ones that hide in the small print — story frames, "raw footage", a round of revisions, exclusivity from competing brands. Price each one. Then add usage (see [Know your usage rights](/resources/usage-rights-before-you-sign.html)). The number you get to is usually higher than the one you'd have said on the phone.
