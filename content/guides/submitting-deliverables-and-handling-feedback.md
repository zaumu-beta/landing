Most delayed approvals come down to how work was submitted, not the work itself. Here's how to hand in a milestone cleanly.

## Submit from the milestone page

Open the campaign, pick the milestone, and click **Submit work**. You can upload files directly (up to 2 GB per file) or paste links to Google Drive, Frame.io, Dropbox or a published post. For links, make sure the brand's reviewers have access before you submit — "requesting access" emails are the number one cause of a lost day.

## Label everything

Name files so a reviewer can find the right one without opening each: `Brand_Campaign_Reel_v2.mp4`, not `final_FINAL2.mp4`. If there are several versions (a 9:16 and a 1:1 crop, say), list them in the submission note.

## Write a short note

The submission note is your chance to steer the review. Three lines is enough: what's included, anything you changed from the brief and why, and what you'd like feedback on. Reviewers who understand your choices approve faster.

## The review window

Once you submit, the brand has the review window from the contract — five business days by default — to approve or request changes. You'll get a notification either way. If the window passes without a response, the milestone is treated as approved and moves to invoicing.

## Handling change requests

Feedback arrives as comments on the milestone, and a change request restarts the review window when you resubmit. Two things keep this painless:

- **Consolidate.** Ask the brand to gather all feedback into one round rather than drip-feeding it; the contract's revision count is per round.
- **Confirm scope.** If a request goes beyond the brief, say so in the thread and offer a change order instead of quietly absorbing it. See [how milestones work](/resources/how-milestone-payments-work.html).

## After approval

Upload your eTIMS invoice, and the milestone moves to payment. Approved work is stored with the campaign, so both you and the brand can always find the final files — and the usage terms that go with them.
